Minimum age. The Service is intended for users aged [13] and over. Where local law sets a higher age of digital consent (for example 16 in some EU countries, or [age] in Vietnam), users below that age need verifiable permission from a parent or guardian, or from their school where the law allows.

Parents who believe their child has used the Service without permission can contact us at [privacy@yourapp.com].

Parental rights. A parent or guardian may, at any time:

  • ask what personal information we hold about their child;
  • ask us to correct or delete that information;
  • withdraw consent and ask us to stop collecting or using their child’s information;
  • ask us to close the child’s account.

We may need to verify your identity and your relationship to the child before acting on a request, to protect the child’s privacy.

Age verification. We ask for date of birth or age range at sign-up. We may use additional checks (such as a parent’s email confirmation or school roster match) where needed. Providing a false age breaches our Terms, and we may suspend the account.

Student defaults. For users under 18, we apply stricter default settings: profiles are not publicly searchable, direct messages from unknown adults are blocked, location sharing is off, and no advertising is shown.

Use through a school. If your school gives you access to the Service:

  • The school may create and manage your account, see your name, class, and account activity, and, if it has enabled this, access group chats it administers.
  • The school decides what information it gives us and why. In those cases the school is responsible for having the right to share it, and we process the information on the school’s instructions under a written agreement.
  • We handle education records only as allowed by that agreement and by laws such as FERPA (United States) or equivalent rules where you live. We do not use student data from schools for any purpose other than providing the Service.
  • Questions about what your school can see or do should go to the school first.

Teachers and adults. Adults in the Service (teachers, moderators) may be subject to extra rules, including background checks required by the school. Adult-to-minor private messaging may be restricted or monitored as described in Section 5.

5. Messages, moderation, and safety

Who can see your messages. Messages are visible to the people in that conversation or group, and to the group’s administrators where the group settings allow it. Once someone receives a message, photo, or file, they can copy, screenshot, or forward it. We cannot control what other users do with content you send them.

Our access to message content. [Set “encryption” at the top of this file to choose one.]

[If messages are end-to-end encrypted:] Messages are encrypted so that only you and the recipients can read them. We cannot read message content, but we can see limited information such as account details, timestamps, and reports that users send us.

[If messages are encrypted in transit and at rest only:] We do not routinely read private messages. We may access message content only when needed to investigate a report, prevent serious harm, comply with law, or maintain the Service.

Reporting and moderation. Any user can report a message, user, or group. When you report content, we may review the reported content and the messages around it, plus related account information. Trained staff or automated tools review reports. We may remove content, warn users, restrict features, suspend accounts, or notify schools, parents, or authorities as described below.

Serious safety risks. We may share information, including message content, with a parent or guardian, a school safeguarding lead, emergency services, or law enforcement if we reasonably believe it is necessary to:

  • prevent or respond to a risk of death or serious harm, including self-harm;
  • protect a child from abuse, grooming, exploitation, or trafficking;
  • respond to threats of violence against a person or school;
  • report child sexual abuse material to the appropriate authority, as required by law.

Bullying and harassment. Bullying, harassment, and hateful content are not allowed. Reported content may be kept longer than ordinary messages (see Section 8) so we can investigate and, if needed, support a school or authority.

Schools and group admins. Teachers or admins who run a class group may see the group’s messages, member lists, and files shared in that group. We tell you in the group info if a group is monitored by a school.

Deleting messages. You can delete your own messages. “Delete for everyone” removes a message from other participants’ view in the App where possible, but copies already saved or screenshotted by others, or held in backups for the period in Section 8, may remain.

Content you post publicly. If the Service offers public or large-group spaces, anything you post there may be seen, copied, and shared by many people, including people outside your school. Think before you post.

7. Data security and what can go wrong

How we protect your information. We use technical and organisational measures suited to the sensitivity of student data, including:

  • encryption of data in transit (TLS) and at rest;
  • hashed and salted password storage, and optional two-factor sign-in;
  • access controls, so staff see only what their role requires, with access logged and reviewed;
  • regular security testing, patching, and vendor reviews;
  • staff confidentiality agreements and privacy and safeguarding training;
  • backups and an incident response plan.

No system is perfectly secure. Despite our efforts, no service can promise absolute security. Risks include hacking, malware, software bugs, outages, human error, insider misuse, and failures at our providers. If you believe your account or information has been compromised, contact us right away at [security@yourapp.com].

If a data breach happens. If a breach is likely to put your information at risk, we will:

  1. contain the incident and investigate its cause and scope;
  2. notify affected users, parents or guardians, and schools without undue delay, and within the time limits that apply (for example 72 hours to a regulator under the GDPR where required);
  3. tell you what happened, what information was involved, what we are doing, and what you can do (such as changing your password);
  4. notify regulators and authorities where the law requires.

Your part. You can help protect your account by using a strong, unique password, not sharing it, signing out on shared devices, keeping your device and App updated, and being careful with links and requests from strangers. We are not responsible for losses caused by you sharing your login with others or by unsafe use of your own device.

Account takeover and impersonation. If someone pretends to be you or gains access to your account, report it to us. We will help you recover access, remove impersonating content, and review the incident.

Service interruptions and data loss. Outages, bugs, or failures can occasionally cause delays, missing messages, or data loss. We keep backups, but we cannot guarantee that every message or file can be recovered. Do not rely on the App as your only copy of important schoolwork.

Depending on where you live, you may have some or all of the rights below. We offer them to every user wherever we reasonably can.

  • Correction: have inaccurate or incomplete information fixed.

  • Portability: receive your information in a common, machine-readable format.
  • Restriction and objection: ask us to limit or stop certain uses, including uses based on our legitimate interests.
  • Withdraw consent: where we rely on consent, you can withdraw it at any time. This does not affect what we did before.
